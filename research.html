My Research.

My research lies on urban and environmental economics, with a broader interest in how cities can become more resilient, accessible, and inclusive.

As cities face growing climate risks, changing patterns of mobility, and persistent housing inequalities, these challenges are increasingly connected. Environmental shocks can disrupt urban activity and deepen existing inequalities, while transport systems and housing markets shape how people access opportunities and adapt to change. I am interested in understanding these interactions and the economic forces behind them, with a focus on how urban systems respond to new challenges and how public policies can improve urban resilience and well-being.

To explore these questions, I use data-driven empirical methods, including econometrics, causal inference, and machine learning, to uncover meaningful patterns, identify causal relationships, and generate evidence that can inform urban policy.

Weathering the Storm: Urban Consumption Responses and Resilience to Tropical Cyclones in China

Ruihua Guo, Binglin Wang

Accepted: IREUS Urban Housing Symposium 2026 (Poster) · CAERE 5th Annual Conference 2026 · CES China Annual Conference 2026

Abstract

This study examines the effects of tropical cyclones on daily urban consumer spending in China, using China UnionPay transaction data from 2011 to 2018 and a stacked difference-in-differences framework. The results show that cyclone landfall leads to sharp declines in both transaction counts and total transaction values, with spending recovering within approximately three days, indicating considerable short-term urban economic resilience. We also find evidence of intertemporal consumption substitution in restaurants, supermarkets, and pharmacies, as residents shift purchases to the days before and after storms. Meanwhile, average transaction values tend to increase during cyclone events, with the strongest effect observed in hospitals. This pattern suggests that although mobility constraints reduce overall transaction activity, urgent purchases and higher-value transactions remain relatively resilient. Information awareness also rises as storms approach, reflected in a significant increase in the Baidu Search Index. Finally, investment in urban water infrastructure, particularly drainage-related infrastructure, appears to mitigate consumption losses and support post-storm recovery, highlighting the role of infrastructure in strengthening urban resilience to climate shocks.

How Subsidized Shared Transport Shapes Urban Mobility Patterns: Evidence from Cellphone Mobility Data in Los Angeles

Ruihua Guo, Jingwei Zhang, Wendong Zhang

Working paper

Abstract

Understanding how emerging shared transport services affect urban mobility is critical for designing sustainable and equitable transit systems. However, empirical evidence on the real-world impacts of subsidized shared transport services remains limited. This study evaluates the impacts of two such services: the Mobility on Demand (MOD) Project and Metro Micro Project, on mobility patterns in Los Angeles County using cellphone-based mobility data. The MOD Project initially utilized a ride-hailing mode with limited semi-fixed routes and transition to point-to-point flexible routes during the COVID-19 pandemic, while the Metro Micro Project adopted a micro-transit mode with mini-buses operating on flexible routes. Applying a matched Difference-in-Differences (DiD) framework, we analyze visit trends at Points of Interest (POIs) across industries and geographic areas from 2018 to 2022. The findings reveal that the MOD Project significantly increased POI visitation major in El Monte area by 7.5%, with the growth primarily from retail trade, food service, and laundry service sectors, and driven by the transition to flexible point-to-point travel. However, the Metro Micro Project had statistically insignificant effects overall, although notable regional heterogeneity was observed. Furthermore, the ride-hailing mode under the MOD Project was more effective in increasing POI visitation than the micro-transit mode of Metro Micro. These results highlight the critical role of service flexibility and mode selection in integrating shared transport with public transit systems as “last-mile solution.”
